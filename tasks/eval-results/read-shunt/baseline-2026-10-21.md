## Reading

- **The go rule is not met.** Whole-file large reads through the `Read` tool
  are concentrated: 38 sessions carry all 58, and the median session has none.
  The 13 sessions over 25% are 4.4% of sessions, a quarter of the bar D11 set.
- **Bare shell reads deliver 5.5× the bytes.** 14.4 MB entered the parent
  through `cat`, `head`, `tail` and `sed -n` against 2.6 MB through large
  `Read` calls — 28.9% of every tool-result byte the parent saw. A gate on the
  `Read` tool alone would miss the larger channel (D6 counted it for this
  reason; the brief's replication bypass used exactly these commands).
- **Sub-agents read more large files than the parent** (160 against 58): the
  delegation the shunt would impose already happens for the heaviest reads.
- **The median context is large regardless** (147.7K tokens, p90 321.9K, 106
  compactions): reads are not what fills it in the typical session.

The gate's remaining tiers stay unbuilt (D9). If the bar moves, the number to
move it with is the shell-read share, not the `Read` share.

## Revision 2026-10-22 — shell-read grammar

The parser applied one option grammar to every shell reader, so `cat -n f`,
`cat -b f` and `cat -s f` swallowed the file as the option's argument and
counted as no shell read (quality-gate finding on `bare_shell_reads`; `head`
and `tail` alone take `-n`/`-c` arguments). The two commands above were re-run
on 2026-10-22 over the same root — 301 sessions by then — with the parser at
the fixed commit and at the one before it:
